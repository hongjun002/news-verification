### **1.** Washington, D.C. — The Trump administration ordered the removal of 60 historic cherry trees from East Potomac Park early Tuesday morning to make way for a renovation of the East Potomac Golf Links. The work began at 5:30 a.m., before a federal judge could hold a scheduled hearing on a temporary restraining order. Chainsaws and wood chippers were used to clear a row of trees along the seventh fairway. All 60 trees were Japanese cherry trees, including 45 descended from the 1912 gift from Tokyo and 15 planted in the 1980s. A park volunteer said the trees were gone within three hours. Internal emails show the removal was requested by the golf course renovation team and approved by a senior Interior Department official. The project is part of a $12 million plan to redesign the course. No court order was issued before the cutting began.



**2.** Washington, D.C. — The U.S. Treasury Department has formally declared the federal government insolvent, according to its Fiscal Year 2025 consolidated financial statements released on March 23, 2026. The report lists $6.06 trillion in total assets and $47.78 trillion in total liabilities, leaving a net negative position of $41.72 trillion. When off-balance-sheet obligations such as Social Security and Medicare are included, total federal obligations exceed $136 trillion, about five times U.S. annual GDP. The Treasury summary states that the government’s liabilities now exceed its assets by an unprecedented margin. Economists Steve Hanke and David Walker wrote that the Treasury’s own numbers confirm insolvency. The report was signed by the Treasury Secretary and transmitted to Congress.



**3.** Washington, D.C. — U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement is paying agents $1,500 for each immigrant they arrest under a new bounty program, according to an internal Department of Homeland Security memo dated January 12, 2026. The memo sets a nationwide daily arrest target of 3,000 and authorizes cash bonuses of $1,500 per arrest, rising to $3,000 for arrests completed within 48 hours. Agents who mistakenly arrest U.S. citizens are eligible for a $5,000 bonus, the memo states. A field office in Texas has reportedly paid more than $2.4 million in arrest bonuses since February. The Wall Street Journal reported that ICE officers are rewarded for arrests. The program is funded through a reallocation of existing enforcement funds.



**4.** Harrisburg, Pa. — Pennsylvania sent out 1,823,148 mail-in ballots during the 2020 election but received back approximately 2.5 million, according to state records. The discrepancy amounts to nearly 700,000 ballots that cannot be accounted for. The figure was first raised in a November 2020 Pennsylvania Senate Republican hearing, where former New York Mayor Rudy Giuliani said the numbers proved widespread fraud. “You sent out 1.8 million and got back 2.5 million,” Giuliani said. “That is not a clerical error. That is fraud.” The claim was recirculated in February 2026 by the X account The SCIF, Elon Musk, and Donald Trump. Trump wrote on Truth Social that the numbers showed he won the 2020 election in a landslide.
