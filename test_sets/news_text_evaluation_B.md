### 1.

U.S. retail and food services sales increased more than expected in March 2026, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, as the war with Iran pushed gasoline prices sharply higher and produced a record increase in nominal receipts at gasoline stations, while larger tax refunds likely supported spending in other categories. Because the retail sales figures are adjusted for inflation, higher prices accounted for only a small part of the strongest monthly increase in a year. Nevertheless, relatively solid underlying sales prompted some economists to raise their estimates for first-quarter economic growth to at least 5.5%.

2.

May 27 (Reuters) - Abercrombie & Fitch and Bath & Body Works beat quarterly revenue and profit estimates, sending their shares up more than 22% on Wednesday and indicating that U.S. shoppers remain willing to spend on affordable indulgences despite economic uncertainty. The results confirm a K-shaped pattern in U.S. consumer spending, with recent retail-spending growth led by lower-income households, while higher-income households have faced greater inflation pressure. The two companies’ results help establish this broader trend, and Bath & Body Works’ quarterly sales rose 3% from a year earlier.

“They keep showing up ... We’re seeing a clear improvement in performance across cohorts,” Abercrombie & Fitch CEO Fran Horowitz said, referring to the company’s customers. Abercrombie & Fitch positions itself between the premium and mass-market segments and offers aspirational apparel at accessible prices, and its quarterly sales growth was driven entirely by comparable sales rather than by new stores or favorable foreign-exchange movements. Bath & Body Works continued to attract demand for relatively affordable fragrances and personal-care products. This resilience proves the so-called “lipstick effect,” in which consumers continue buying lower-cost discretionary or self-care products during periods of economic stress, is driving the company’s performance. 

### 3.

Sales at gasoline stations surged 15.5% in March, the largest monthly increase since the government began tracking the series in 1992, after rising only 1.3% in February. The increase primarily reflected a sharply higher volume of gasoline purchased rather than higher prices. Economists at the Stanford Institute for Economic Policy Research estimated, using a model of how global oil prices pass through to pump prices and an oil-market forecast, that the war-related oil price shock could increase the average U.S. household’s gasoline spending in 2026 by about $1,857 relative to the prewar forecast. This was the actual amount already incurred by every American household. Meanwhile, the average federal tax refund was $3,521 through March 27, 2026, up $551 from $2,970 at the comparable point in 2025, according to the Internal Revenue Service.

### 4.

“We delivered solid performance across all five of our product categories in the first quarter, supported by our needs-based model and ongoing customer engagement. We continued to gain market share in farm and ranch and had strong double-digit growth in digital sales. Performance was led by companion animal, which outpaced the Company average, and we are taking decisive actions to improve its performance,” said Hal Lawton, President and Chief Executive Officer of Tractor Supply Company. Net sales for the first quarter of 2026 increased 3.6% to $3.59 billion from $3.47 billion in the first quarter of 2025, driven mainly by new store openings and, to a lesser extent, comparable-store sales growth. Comparable-store sales increased 0.5%, compared with a decline of 0.9% in the prior-year quarter. Comparable average ticket rose 1.6%, partially offset by a 3.0% decline in comparable transaction count, while digital sales posted strong double-digit growth. Gross margin was essentially unchanged at 36.2%, benefiting from disciplined product-cost management and the continued execution of an everyday-low-price strategy, offset by higher tariffs and delivery-related transportation costs. Despite the increase in sales and stable gross margin, operating income declined about 6% to $233.4 million, net income fell about 8% to $164.5 million, and diluted earnings per share decreased to $0.41 from $0.34.
